# Vision: speak English naturally, like a native

Every feature in TOEFL Voice Coach and TOEFL Pocket Coach is judged by one question:

> **Does this make the learner's English sound more natural and more like a native speaker's?**

A TOEFL score is a checkpoint along the way. The goal is English the learner can use comfortably in real life.

## Teaching principles

1. **Natural beats merely correct.** Feedback separates two things: *correct* ("It is very delicious food") and *natural* ("It's sooo good"). Always show the version a native speaker would actually say.
2. **Learn chunks, not single words.** Teach collocations, phrasal verbs and whole expressions (*have an impact on*, *figure it out*), not isolated dictionary words.
3. **Hear it before you say it.** Listening comes first, then shadowing: repeat along with native audio, copying stress, rhythm, linking and reductions (*gonna*, *wanna*, *turn it off → tur-ni-doff*).
4. **Use it about your own life.** Every new word or phrase ends with the learner saying a sentence about their own life. Memorizing without speaking doesn't count.
5. **Recycle until it sticks.** Words and native phrases from feedback come back through spaced repetition until the learner uses them without thinking.
6. **Level up with i+1.** Content sits one step above the learner's current level (A2 → B1 → B2 → C1). The "better version" of an answer is always written one level higher.
7. **Daily life and TOEFL together.** Every item has to earn its place on both sides: it appears on the TOEFL *and* people use it in everyday conversation.
8. **Positive, light coaching.** The coach is cheerful and encouraging. Mistakes are normal steps in learning, never failures.

## Checklist for every new feature

- [ ] Does it show the natural, native way to say it, not just the correct one?
- [ ] Does the learner speak, or only read?
- [ ] Is it one level above where the learner is now?
- [ ] Will the learner see it again later (spaced review)?
- [ ] Is it useful both on the TOEFL and in daily life?
